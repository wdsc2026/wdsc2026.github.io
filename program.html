09:10–09:50 Invited talk 1: Functional Encryption for Multiple Users, with Public Inputs and Strong Security Speaker Ky Nguyen · Sorbonne Université

Functional Encryption (FE) is a powerful cryptographic primitive that allows for fine-grained computation over encrypted data. Notions of FE in the multi-user setting (Goldwasser et al. at Eurocrypt’14; Chotard et al. at Asiacrypt’18) such as Multi-Client FE (MCFE) and Multi-Input FE (MIFE) become the subjects of a plethora of works, which study on concrete function classes, improving security, finding practical privacy-preserving applications, and more. The challenge becomes more important when combined with access control, such as attribute-based encryption (ABE), which was actually not covered syntactically by the public-key FE nor semantically by the secret-key MCFE frameworks. On the other hand, as for complex primitives, many works have studied the admissibility of adversaries to ensure that the security model encompasses all real threats of attacks.

In this talk, we explore new points of view, stemming from the strong admissibility, that eventually encompass public-key FE and classical ABE, bridging the private setting of MCFE with the public setting of FE and ABE.

Moreover, focusing on security notion, current security notions so far only consider the chosen-plaintext attacks and stay vulnerable to active attacks. We look into the chosen-ciphertext security in multi-user FE, giving a robust notion that is accompanied by generic transformation under the above new viewpoints.

All above constructions can be instantiated for concrete and practical function classes under standard assumptions.

09:50–10:30 Invited talk 2: Tulva: Practical Asynchronous Threshold FHE Speaker Russell W. F. Lai · Aalto University

Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) enables computation on encrypted data, but a single secret key creates a single point of failure. Tulva distributes decryption across a threshold of parties, allowing them to submit shares independently without coordination or simultaneous availability. It adds asynchronous threshold decryption to existing FHE schemes while largely preserving their ciphertexts. The construction relies on the standard LWE assumption and includes an open-source implementation evaluated for privacy-preserving Ethereum Virtual Machine applications.

11:00–11:40 Invited talk 3: The AI Money, The Endgame: Introduction to Pearl Blockchain Speaker Youlong Ding · Hebrew University of Jerusalem

Cryptocurrency and AI are often seen as competing for GPU resources. Pearl blockchain offers an elegant way to reconcile the two by leveraging GPU computations for AI inference as proof of work to secure a blockchain. This talk provides a brief introduction to Pearl's Proof of Useful Work (PoUW) protocol.

Invited Speakers

Youlong Ding

Hebrew University of Jerusalem

Youlong Ding is a graduate student at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. His current research interest lies in complexity, cryptography, and AI.

Russell W. F. Lai

Aalto University

Russell W. F. Lai has been an Assistant Professor at the Department of Computer Science of Aalto University since July 2022, where he is part of the Aalto Crypto Group. Before joining Aalto, he obtained his PhD from Friedrich-Alexander University Erlangen-Nuremberg in 2022 (dissertation), and his MPhil and bachelor’s degrees from The Chinese University of Hong Kong.

Ky Nguyen (Nguyễn Ngọc Kỷ)

Sorbonne Université

Ky Nguyen (Nguyễn Ngọc Kỷ) is an Associate Professor and a permanent member of the ALMASTY research team at Sorbonne University. He received his PhD from École Normale Supérieure in December 2024, under the supervision of David Pointcheval and Duong Hieu Phan. His research appears in CCS, CRYPTO, ASIACRYPT, PKC covering advanced public-key encryption and signatures, with a focus on removing trusted centralized point of failure, together with privacy-preserving techniques in computation and verification.

Ky Nguyen also co-chaired the recent IACR-VIASM Summer School on Cryptography 2026 in Hanoi, Vietnam, aiming to promote cryptographic research in Vietnam and neighboring countries.
